This guide is about the part of trip planning that decides how a Vietnam holiday actually feels: how many stops to attempt, which order to do them in, what each route costs, and when to go. Everything is written for first-time visitors, in plain English, with prices shown as ranges we review rather than promises.

Start with a route

Most people arrive with between five and ten days. The three itineraries below cover that range: a compact loop around the north, a one-way run from Hanoi to Hoi An, and a full north-to-south trip for anyone who wants the highlights in one go. Each one lists the days in order, how long the transfers take, where to sleep in each stop and what the whole thing costs at three comfort levels.

Work out what it will cost

Vietnam can be a $45-a-day trip or a $250-a-day trip without changing a single destination. The trip budget calculator takes your dates, group size, comfort level and the places you plan to sleep, then shows a low and a high figure for accommodation, food, local transport, activities and the journeys in between. It runs entirely in your browser and stores nothing.

A word on prices. Every figure on this site is a planning estimate in US dollars, reviewed in September 2026. Vietnam's travel prices move with the season and with how far ahead you book, so treat the low end as a good week booked early and the high end as a realistic buffer for busy dates.

How this guide is made

The routes, costs and practical notes here are written by the team at Vibe Vietnam Tour, a licensed tour operator based in Hanoi, and they are written separately from that company's tour pages: nothing on this site is copied from it, and no hotel or booking site pays to be mentioned.
