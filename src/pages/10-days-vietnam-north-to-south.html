Ten days is enough to see the three parts of Vietnam that first-time visitors come for: Hanoi and the limestone bays of the north, the old trading town of Hoi An in the centre, and Ho Chi Minh City with the Mekong Delta in the south. This route uses two short internal flights and one night on a boat in Lan Ha Bay. It moves at a steady pace rather than a sprint, but it does move. You will repack every few days, you will pass through three airports, and you will leave out the northern mountains.

Who this ten-day route suits

This itinerary is built for first-time visitors who want the classic highlights and accept two internal flights to reach them. It suits travellers who are comfortable being somewhere new every second or third day, who can handle one long transfer day, and who would rather see three regions properly than five regions badly.

It also works well for couples and friends travelling together, and for families with teenagers. Two people sharing a room get noticeably better value everywhere in Vietnam, because almost all hotel rooms are priced per room rather than per person.

It is a poor fit if you want to stay put. There is no real beach time beyond an afternoon in Hoi An, no trekking, and no time in the far north around Ha Giang or Sapa. If you like slow mornings and long lunches, read the section on slowing the route down before you book anything.

Why fly into Hanoi and out of Ho Chi Minh City

Vietnam is long and thin. Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City sit at opposite ends of the country, and a return ticket in and out of the same city forces you either to double back or to skip a region entirely.

An open-jaw ticket, flying into Hanoi and home from Ho Chi Minh City, solves that. You travel in one direction and never repeat a leg. On this route it saves roughly a full day of travel and one internal flight compared with flying home from Hanoi, because you would otherwise need a third flight, or a thirty-hour train ride, to get back to the north.

When you search, use the multi-city option rather than return. Open-jaw fares from Europe, North America and Australia are often close to the price of an ordinary return to one city, and sometimes identical, though the very cheapest return deals can still beat them. Check both before you decide, and allow a buffer of a few hours between your domestic arrival and your international departure on the way home.

Two internal flights do the work here: Hanoi to Da Nang, and Da Nang to Ho Chi Minh City. Both are short hops with frequent daily departures and both are inexpensive if you book a few weeks ahead. When to take the train instead

The Reunification Express runs the length of the country, and the sleeper carriages are a genuine part of the trip rather than a compromise. A soft sleeper from Hanoi to Hue costs about $30–55 (estimate, September 2026), Hanoi to Da Nang around $35–65, and Da Nang to Ho Chi Minh City roughly $45–80.

Take the train instead of flying when:

  • you have twelve days or more and can absorb a slower leg without cutting a place you care about;
  • you want the coastal section between Hue and Da Nang, which the railway does far better than a plane;
  • you are travelling over Tet or another national holiday, when domestic airfares rise sharply and airports are at their busiest;
  • you would rather sleep through the journey and save a hotel night, even though the train is slower door to door.

Do not put the train on the Hanoi to Da Nang leg of this ten-day plan. It takes the better part of a day or a full night, and it will cost you either a day in Hoi An or the night on the bay boat. If you drop the Mekong Delta day trip, an overnight train south becomes realistic. For the short link between Hue and Hoi An, a sleeper bus costs about $15–30 (estimate, September 2026) and takes around four hours.

Best months to go: the north and the south run on different clocks

No single month is ideal across all three regions, because the north and the south sit under different monsoon cycles. Decide which region matters most to you, then check what the others are doing in the same weeks.

The north: Hanoi, Lan Ha Bay, Ninh Binh

October to April is the cooler, drier half of the year. November and the stretch from late March into April are usually the most comfortable weeks. December to February is genuinely cold by Vietnamese standards, often grey and drizzly, and the bays can be misty, which is atmospheric but not the postcard most people have in mind.

May to September is hot and humid with heavy afternoon rain. August and September carry the highest typhoon risk of the year, and that is when bay cruises are most likely to be rerouted or cancelled at short notice.

The centre: Hue, Da Nang, Hoi An

February to August is the dry stretch. It turns hot from June onwards and the sea is warm and swimmable. September to November is the wet season: Hue and Hoi An flood in some years, and October and November bring the strongest typhoon risk. If your trip depends on Hoi An being dry, avoid late September and October.

The south: Ho Chi Minh City and the Mekong Delta

December to April is the dry season, hot and mostly clear. May to November is the wet season, but the rain usually arrives in short, heavy bursts in the afternoon rather than as all-day drizzle, and mornings are normally usable. The delta is greenest and busiest with fruit in the wet months.

If you want one window that works reasonably well everywhere, aim for late February to early April, or late October into November if you accept rain in the centre.

The plan, day by day

Nine nights in total: two in Hanoi, one on the boat, three in Hoi An, two in Ho Chi Minh City, and one more on day seven. Day seven is the flexible one and it decides the shape of the rest. Spend it as a Hue day trip and the ninth night stays in Hoi An; stay overnight in Hue and you swap that Hoi An night for a guesthouse near the citadel. The day numbering below assumes you land in Hanoi in the morning or early afternoon.

Day 1 — Arrive in Hanoi

Land at Noi Bai and take a Grab or a pre-booked private car into the city; a Grab airport transfer runs about $12–25 and a private car about $20–35 (estimates, September 2026). The drive takes forty to sixty minutes depending on traffic. Check in, then walk rather than nap. Start at Hoan Kiem Lake, cross the red bridge to Ngoc Son Temple, and drift north into the Old Quarter, where the lanes still loosely follow their old trades. Keep the first evening simple: a bowl of pho, a beer on a plastic stool, an early night.

Day 2 — Hanoi in depth

Hanoi rewards a full day on foot. In the morning, visit the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex and the One Pillar Pagoda nearby, then walk to the Temple of Literature. Check opening days locally, because the mausoleum closes on some weekdays and for annual maintenance. Lunch at a neighbourhood place rather than a hotel. In the afternoon, choose the Museum of Ethnology or Hoa Lo Prison, then loop around Truc Bach and West Lake. Finish with a water puppet show and dinner in the Old Quarter. Confirm your bay transfer for tomorrow before you go to bed.

Day 3 — Hanoi to Lan Ha Bay, board the boat

An early pickup takes you east to the coast, roughly two and a half to three hours to the harbour. Boarding is usually late morning, with lunch served on the way out, kayaking or a swim in the afternoon, and dinner on deck as the limestone towers turn dark. Lan Ha Bay sits south of Ha Long Bay and shares the same scenery with a fraction of the boat traffic. Read what your cruise includes before booking: meals, kayaks, a cave visit and the road transfer are often in the price, while drinks usually are not. You sleep on board.

Day 4 — Bay morning, then south to Hoi An

Sunrise on the water, often with a short paddle or a visit to a floating fishing village, then brunch and disembark around midday. Drive back towards Hanoi and take a late afternoon or early evening flight to Da Nang, followed by a forty-five minute transfer to Hoi An. This is the longest day of the trip, and the one day worth booking as a single package rather than improvising leg by leg.

If the idea of a bay morning followed by a flight sounds exhausting, add one night in Hanoi at the end of the cruise and fly the following morning. That pushes the whole plan back by a day, so take the night from Ho Chi Minh City or from the Mekong day trip.

Day 5 — Hoi An: the old town and the river

Start early, before the tour groups arrive and the heat builds. A single combined ticket covers several of the old merchant houses, the assembly halls and the covered bridge. Then pick a tailor and order something, because a first fitting is usually possible the same evening. In the afternoon, cycle out past the rice fields or take the short ride to An Bang beach. Come back for the lanterns along the river, a bowl of cao lau, and a boat trip if you want one.

Day 6 — Hoi An: countryside and coast

Choose one proper activity and keep the rest of the day free. A cooking class with a market visit is the most popular option; a cycling loop through the herb gardens and the coconut palms is the most relaxed; the Cham temple ruins at My Son are the most historically interesting, but they are about eighty minutes away each way, so leave early. Whatever you pick, protect the late afternoon for the pool or the beach. This is the day that stops the trip feeling like a checklist.

Day 7 — Hue, as a day trip or a night away

The drive north over the Hai Van Pass takes around three hours by private car, which costs about $60–110 per car (estimate, September 2026), and it is worth doing in daylight for the views over the coast. In Hue, the Imperial Citadel is the main sight, with the royal tombs spread along the Perfume River; a boat or a motorbike with a driver is the easy way to link them. You can return to Hoi An the same evening, which makes for a long day, or stay one night and continue to Da Nang airport the next morning. If you have had enough temples, skip Hue and take a beach day instead.

Day 8 — Fly to Ho Chi Minh City

Transfer to Da Nang airport, about forty-five minutes from Hoi An and considerably longer from Hue, for a flight of roughly an hour and twenty minutes. From Tan Son Nhat, take a Grab or a private car to your hotel and spend the afternoon in District 1: the War Remnants Museum, the Reunification Palace, the central post office and the book street beside it, then a walk down to the river. Dinner is the main event here. The city's food is the single best reason to have come south.

Day 9 — Mekong Delta day trip

A guided day tour costs about $35–70 per person (estimate, September 2026), leaves early and returns in the late afternoon. Most run to My Tho or Ben Tre and include a boat on the main river, a narrow canal rowed by hand, a coconut or honey stop, lunch, and a fair amount of time in a minibus. It is a taste of the delta rather than the real thing. If you want the real thing, replace it with two days and a night in Can Tho or Ben Tre and cut a day elsewhere.

Day 10 — Ho Chi Minh City and departure

Use the morning for whatever you missed: a coffee on a rooftop, the Fine Arts Museum, or a last wander through the market. Then build in a traffic buffer. Allow at least an hour to the airport in the middle of the day, more in the evening rush, and be there two to three hours before an international flight. If your flight home leaves late at night, ask for a late checkout or book a day-use room rather than sitting in the terminal all afternoon.

Where to stay, by area and price band

Vietnamese hotels price by room, not by person, so two people sharing pay roughly half of the figures below. As a guide: a hostel dorm bed is $7–14 per bed, a family guesthouse or two-star hotel $18–35 per room, a mid-range three-star $40–75, a comfortable four-star $90–150, and a five-star $170–380 (all estimates, September 2026).

Hanoi

  • Old Quarter — the most atmosphere and the most noise; everything is walkable and the street food is right outside.
  • Around Hoan Kiem Lake — quieter at night, still central, slightly higher prices.
  • Ba Dinh and Truc Bach — more local, calmer, fifteen to twenty minutes from the Old Quarter by taxi.

Lan Ha Bay

Choose by cabin and route rather than by brand name. Mid-range cabins on a one-night cruise generally run $170–260 per person and comfortable cabins $260–350 (estimates, September 2026). Ask specifically what is included: transfers, all meals, kayaking and any cave or village visit.

Hoi An

  • Riverside, in the old town — beautiful at night and convenient, but can be damp in the wet season and noisy until late.
  • Between the old town and An Bang beach — small hotels with pools, bicycles provided, ten minutes from both.
  • An Bang — beach first, town second; quieter, and best if you mainly want the sea.

Hue

Stay south of the Perfume River if you want the citadel and the restaurants on foot. The Phu Hoi area has the densest cluster of cheap guesthouses and hostels.

Ho Chi Minh City

  • District 1, around Dong Khoi and Le Thanh Ton — walkable, close to the sights, the most expensive part of the city.
  • The Pham Ngu Lao area — cheap rooms and hostels, loud until the early hours.
  • District 3 — quieter, five to ten minutes away by taxi, better value for mid-range hotels.

How to slow this down if ten days feels rushed

Ten days across three regions is comfortable rather than frantic, but it is not a rest. If you read the plan and feel tired, there are four honest ways to take pressure out of it without adding days.

  1. Drop the Mekong Delta day trip. It is the longest single day of the trip after day four, and it is the easiest thing to remove. Keep Ho Chi Minh City as an arrival and departure base with one full day in the city, and give the spare day to Hoi An.
  2. Skip Hue. The round trip from Hoi An is a long day in a car. If you are not specifically interested in imperial history, use day seven for the beach, a cooking class or a second tailor fitting.
  3. Add one night in Hanoi and use it for Ninh Binh. Two nights in Hanoi is the minimum, but a third night lets you spend a full day among the limestone valleys south of the city without changing hotels. This is the single best addition if you have one spare day.
  4. Turn one flight into a train. An overnight soft sleeper on the Da Nang to Ho Chi Minh City leg makes the journey part of the trip and saves a hotel night, though it costs you a morning.

The wider fix is to stop treating the number of places as the goal. If you have twelve days, add Ninh Binh and a night in the delta instead of a fourth city. If you have fourteen, the same route with an extra night in Hoi An and a two-day Mekong loop feels unhurried. The days you remember are rarely the ones you spent in an airport.

Frequently asked questions

Is ten days enough to see Vietnam properly?

Enough to see three regions well, not enough to see the country. You get Hanoi, the northern bays, Hoi An with a side trip to Hue, and Ho Chi Minh City with a taste of the Mekong Delta. What you miss is the far north, the central highlands and the beaches south of Nha Trang. Treat it as a first visit, not a complete one.

Should I book the two internal flights before I arrive?

Book them a few weeks ahead if your dates are fixed, especially for the Hanoi to Da Nang leg, which is the busiest of the two. Domestic fares on these routes swing widely, and the cheapest seats go first. If your plans are loose, you can usually buy a ticket a day or two before at a higher price, since both routes have frequent daily departures.

Ha Long Bay or Lan Ha Bay, which should I choose?

They share the same limestone scenery. Ha Long Bay is the famous name and has the most boats, which means more company on the water. Lan Ha Bay, to the south, is quieter, and many of the smaller cruises sail there instead. For a single overnight, Lan Ha Bay usually gives you a calmer experience for the same money.

Can I run this route in reverse, starting in Ho Chi Minh City?

Yes, and it works just as well. Fly into Ho Chi Minh City, do the Mekong Delta first, fly to Da Nang, spend time in Hoi An and Hue, then take the boat in Lan Ha Bay and finish in Hanoi. The open-jaw logic is identical. The only real difference is the weather window you land in at each end.

Is Hue better as a day trip from Hoi An or as an overnight stay?

If you care about the citadel and the royal tombs, stay one night. The drive is around three hours each way, so a day trip means six hours in a car for four or five hours of sightseeing. If you are mainly curious, a long day trip is fine. An overnight also lets you leave for Da Nang airport the next morning instead of doubling back.

What is the most tiring day, and how do I make it easier?

Day four, when you leave the boat, drive back towards Hanoi, fly to Da Nang and transfer to Hoi An. Book it as one coordinated package with a private car rather than separate pieces, take the latest sensible flight rather than the earliest, and keep the next morning free. Adding a night in Hanoi before the flight is the comfortable alternative.

Can I fit Ninh Binh into this itinerary?

Only by adding a day. The usual fix is a third night in Hanoi, with a full day trip south to the limestone valleys, which means taking that night from Ho Chi Minh City or dropping the Mekong Delta tour. Ninh Binh deserves at least one full day and is often described as Ha Long Bay on land.
