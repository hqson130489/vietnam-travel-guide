Five days in the north works only if you accept one trade-off: you change beds three times, and one of those changes means a four-hour drive to the coast. In exchange you get the Old Quarter on foot, the limestone valleys of Ninh Binh by bicycle, and a night anchored between the karst islands of Lan Ha Bay. This plan shows the realistic travel times, what to book before you fly, and what the whole thing costs at three different budgets.

Who this route suits, and who should skip it

This is a good first trip to Vietnam. It covers three distinct landscapes without a single domestic flight, and the longest transfer is short enough to sleep through. If you want to come home feeling you have seen the north rather than just Hanoi, this is the shortest honest version of that trip.

It suits you if you are comfortable moving every night or two, you do not mind a 7am departure, and you would rather see four places properly than six badly. It also works well for couples and for friends travelling together, because the cost of a private car or a mid-range cabin splits cleanly in two.

Skip it, or change it, if any of these apply

  • You want Sapa, Mai Chau or the Ha Giang loop as well. Those need their own three to four days each. Adding them to this route turns five days into a blur of van seats.
  • You are travelling with a baby or a toddler. Three hotel changes and a long coastal drive are hard work with small children; two bases would be kinder.
  • You land in Hanoi after about 10pm, or leave before about 6am. You lose most of Day 1 or Day 5, and the plan stops making sense.
  • You are coming between December and February and you hate damp cold. Hanoi can sit around 12–18C with drizzle for days, and the bay can be grey.
  • You want one hotel and day trips only. Choose Hanoi plus Ninh Binh, or Hanoi plus the bay, and drop the third stop.

If you would rather have the route shaped around your dates and pace, tailor-made Vietnam itineraries are the quickest way to see which swaps are realistic for your arrival and departure times.

Best months for northern Vietnam

The north has four genuinely different seasons, and the bay is the part most sensitive to weather.

October to November

The best window overall. Hanoi cools to roughly 20–28C, rain becomes occasional rather than constant, and the bay is often clear with calm water. Rice around Ninh Binh is golden in the first half of October in many years. Book the boat early, because this is when everyone else wants it too.

March to April

The second good window. Temperatures climb back to the mid twenties, there is still some drizzle and low cloud, and the countryside is green and full of water. Fewer visitors than October, and the bay is usually workable, though misty mornings are common.

December to February

Cool and often damp. Hanoi sits around 12–18C. The bay is quieter and cheaper, but views can be flat grey and some boats reduce their routes. Pack a real jacket, not a hoodie.

May to September

Hot, humid and wet. Hanoi regularly reaches 33–38C, afternoon storms are common from June, and the coast carries typhoon risk from roughly July to September. Boats do get cancelled for safety, sometimes with a day of notice. If you travel now, keep the bay night in the middle of the trip rather than at the end, so a cancellation can be reshuffled.

Two dates to check before booking: Vietnamese Lunar New Year (Tet), which falls in late January or February depending on the year, and the long public holiday around 30 April and 1 May. Domestic travel peaks, prices rise, and some family-run places close for several days.

Day-by-day plan

Day 1 — Arrive in Hanoi, sleep in the Old Quarter

Travel time: Noi Bai airport to the Old Quarter is 30–50 minutes by car, depending on traffic. A metered Grab or a pre-booked private car costs $12–25 and $20–35 respectively (estimate, September 2026).

Do not plan anything ambitious for arrival day. Drop your bag, walk to Hoan Kiem Lake and circle it once, cross the red bridge to Ngoc Son Temple, then let yourself get mildly lost in the streets north of the lake. The Old Quarter is organised loosely by trade, so you will walk past rows of tin-box makers, then shoe shops, then silk.

Eat: bun cha for dinner, grilled pork with noodles and herbs, or a bowl of pho if you want something gentler after a flight. Finish with an egg coffee, which is exactly what it sounds like: strong coffee under a thick custard of whipped yolk. The lakefront and the streets around it are dense with small family restaurants; look for the ones with plastic stools and a single dish on the menu.

Book ahead: airport transfer, first two hotel nights, and a water puppet theatre ticket if you want one. The theatre is small and sells out on weekends.

Day 2 — A full day in Hanoi, second night in the city

Keep this day in Hanoi rather than rushing to Ninh Binh. It is the difference between seeing the city and driving past it.

Morning: start at the Temple of Literature, Vietnam's first university, then walk or take a short taxi to the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex and the One Pillar Pagoda. The mausoleum keeps limited opening hours and closes for annual maintenance, so confirm the days locally before you build your morning around it. Dress respectfully: shoulders and knees covered.

Afternoon: pick one museum rather than three. The Museum of Ethnology is the strongest for understanding the country's ethnic groups, and Hoa Lo Prison is the most affecting for twentieth-century history. If museums are not your thing, walk the shore of West Lake and the quieter Truc Bach peninsula instead, and stop for coffee.

Eat: cha ca, turmeric-marinated fish cooked at the table with dill and spring onion, is the Hanoi dish most visitors miss. Banh cuon, steamed rice rolls, makes a cheap breakfast. For dinner, the streets just east of Hoan Kiem have dozens of small kitchens.

Book ahead: a limousine van seat for tomorrow morning. These are 9 to 11 seat vans with reclining seats, and the good departure times go first. Hanoi to Ninh Binh is $12–20 per person (estimate, September 2026).

Day 3 — Hanoi to Ninh Binh, then boats and limestone

Travel time: 2 to 2.5 hours by limousine van, roughly 120km south. Leave between 7:30 and 8:00 and you will be in Tam Coc before lunch.

Ninh Binh is often called the inland Ha Long Bay, which undersells it. The same steep limestone towers rise straight out of flat rice fields, but here you move through them by rowing boat and bicycle rather than by ship.

Afternoon: choose one boat trip, not both. Trang An is the larger site, about three hours, with several cave passages and temple stops, and it is the one to pick if you only do one. Tam Coc is shorter, around two hours, and the rowers often use their feet on the oars, which is worth seeing once. After the boat, hire a bicycle and ride the lane to Bich Dong pagoda and out into the villages.

Late afternoon: climb the steps at Hang Mua for the view over the Tam Coc valley. There are around 500 steps, it takes 20 to 30 minutes up in the heat, and late afternoon light is better than midday. Skip it if it has rained heavily.

Eat: Ninh Binh is known for goat meat, crispy burnt rice, and mountain snails. Any family restaurant around Tam Coc will serve all three, and the set meals are generous.

Book ahead: the van, one night of accommodation, and your boat ticket if you are travelling on a weekend or a public holiday.

Day 4 — Ninh Binh to Lan Ha Bay, night on the boat

Travel time: 3 to 3.5 hours by road from Ninh Binh to the Got ferry pier area near Hai Phong. Leave by 7:30 to be at the pier for a noon boarding.

Lan Ha Bay is the southern half of the same limestone archipelago as Ha Long Bay, behind Cat Ba island. It has the same scenery with a fraction of the boat traffic, which matters more than any brochure photograph suggests.

On the water: boarding is usually around 12:00 to 12:30, with lunch served as the boat moves out. A typical afternoon includes kayaking through a floating fishing village, a swim stop at one of the small beaches, and a visit to Viet Hai village on Cat Ba if your boat runs that route. Dinner is on board, and the evening is genuinely dark and quiet.

Book ahead: this is the one booking to lock in first. Cabins on a mid-range boat are $170–260 per person (estimate, September 2026) including meals, kayaking and the cave or village stop. Across the fleet, cabins run $130–350 per person (estimate, September 2026), so the cabin band you choose is the single biggest lever on your trip total. Ask three questions before you pay: does the boat actually cruise Lan Ha rather than only Ha Long, are kayaks included or charged separately, and what happens if the harbour authority cancels the sailing for weather.

Day 5 — Bay sunrise, back to Hanoi, fly out

Mornings on the bay are the best part. Many boats offer a short tai chi session on the sun deck at sunrise, followed by a light breakfast and one last stop: a cave, a kayak, or a beach depending on the route.

Disembark: usually between 10:30 and 11:00. The drive back to Hanoi takes 2.5 to 3 hours, so you reach the city around 14:00 to 14:30.

That leaves a useful half day. Leave your luggage at your hotel or a cafe, do a last loop of the Old Quarter for coffee and gifts, and consider a foot massage before the flight. If you are flying out the same evening, do not book a departure earlier than about 18:00 from Noi Bai, and allow the full 30 to 50 minutes of city traffic plus two hours at the airport. If your flight leaves early the next morning, sleep near the airport instead of in the city.

Where to stay in each stop, by price band

Prices below are per room per night for two people, except dorm beds and the bay cabins, which are per person.

Hanoi

Stay in the Old Quarter or around Hoan Kiem Lake for your first visit. Everything is walkable, the food is best there, and the airport transfer is short. West Lake and Tay Ho are quieter and more residential, about 15 to 20 minutes away by taxi. Ba Dinh suits anyone who wants the museums on their doorstep.

  • Budget: dorm bed $7–14 per bed, or a family guesthouse or 2-star room $18–35 (estimate, September 2026)
  • Mid-range: 3-star hotel $40–75 (estimate, September 2026)
  • Comfort: 4-star $90–150, 5-star $170–380 (estimate, September 2026)

Ninh Binh

Tam Coc is the practical choice: guesthouses and homestays sit in the rice fields, restaurants are within walking distance, and bicycles come with the room. Trang An is quieter and greener. Ninh Binh city is cheapest and best for onward buses, but you will need a taxi for everything.

  • Budget: dorm bed $7–14 per bed, or a family guesthouse $18–35 (estimate, September 2026)
  • Mid-range: 3-star hotel or a good homestay $40–75 (estimate, September 2026)
  • Comfort: 4-star or resort-style $90–150 (estimate, September 2026). There are very few true 5-star options here.

Lan Ha Bay

On this night, the boat is your accommodation, so the cabin category is the decision. Cheaper boats are smaller, with simpler cabins and more tightly packed schedules. Mid-range boats have larger cabins, better food and fewer passengers. Premium boats add balconies, better guides and quieter routes.

  • Budget cabin: $130–170 per person (estimate, September 2026)
  • Mid-range cabin: $170–260 per person (estimate, September 2026)
  • Comfort cabin: $260–350 per person (estimate, September 2026)

All cabin rates normally include meals, kayaking and the scheduled stops, and exclude drinks and tips.

If you have one extra day

One extra day changes the trip more than you would expect, because it removes the tightest transfer. The best use of it depends on what you found frustrating.

Add a second night in Ninh Binh

The most rewarding option. A full extra day lets you ride out to Van Long, a quieter wetland reserve with the same limestone scenery and far fewer boats, or spend a morning in Cuc Phuong National Park, Vietnam's oldest national park, with its primate rescue centre and easy forest trails. You could also cycle the back lanes past Bich Dong and Thai Vi temple without watching the clock.

Add a night on Cat Ba island

Sleep on land between Ninh Binh and the bay, then board the boat the following morning. Cat Ba town has a waterfront strip and easy access to the national park, and it breaks the long drive into two shorter ones.

Add a day in Hanoi

Half-day trips out of the city work well: the ceramics village of Bat Trang, or Duong Lam, a village of laterite houses and old communal halls. A cooking class with a market visit is the other obvious choice, and it pairs well with a rainy day.

Or go further, if you have two extra days

Two extra days opens up Mai Chau for rice-field valleys and Thai minority villages, or a short Sapa stay if you accept the night train or the expressway bus. Both are better as their own trip than as an addition to this one, but two days is the minimum at which they become worth the travel.

Frequently asked questions

Is five days enough to see northern Vietnam properly?

Enough for a genuine first taste, not for the whole north. This route covers Hanoi, Ninh Binh and Lan Ha Bay at a reasonable pace, with two full days in Hanoi. It does not include Sapa, Ha Giang or Mai Chau, and it should not try to. If you want mountains as well, plan seven to nine days instead.

Should I book the Lan Ha Bay boat before I arrive?

Yes, for October, November, March and April, and for any weekend or public holiday. Good mid-range boats sell out weeks ahead and the price only moves one way. Booking two to six weeks ahead is usually enough in low season. If you are flexible on dates, leaving it to the last minute can occasionally get you a discount, but not in peak months.

Lan Ha Bay or Ha Long Bay, which should I choose?

Lan Ha Bay for the same scenery with far fewer boats, which makes kayaking and swimming noticeably calmer. Ha Long Bay if you want the classic postcard route and the widest choice of ships and prices. Both leave from the same general area near Hai Phong. Many boats advertised as Ha Long actually cruise Lan Ha, so check the route rather than the label.

Can I do this route independently without a tour?

Yes. Limousine vans and Grab cover every leg, guesthouses and hotels are easy to book directly, and boat tickets at Trang An and Tam Coc are bought at the entrance. You will need to be comfortable arranging your own transfers the night before, and the Ninh Binh to coast leg needs a booked car or van rather than a roadside hail. A driver for that one leg removes most of the stress.

Is Ninh Binh worth an overnight stay, or can I day-trip it from Hanoi?

An overnight is much better. A day trip from Hanoi gives you one boat ride and a rushed climb, plus four to five hours in a vehicle. Staying one night lets you do Trang An in the afternoon, Hang Mua in good light, and the villages by bicycle the next morning before the tour buses arrive. It is also the cheapest night of this itinerary.

What happens if bad weather cancels my bay cruise?

The harbour authority can suspend sailings when winds or a typhoon make the bay unsafe, usually with a day or two of notice. Reputable operators rebook you onto the next available sailing or refund the cruise. This is the strongest argument for placing the bay night in the middle of your trip rather than on your final night, and for keeping one buffer day if you travel between July and September.
