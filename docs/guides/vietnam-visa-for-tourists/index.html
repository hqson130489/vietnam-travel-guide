Vietnam Visa for Tourists: How the Process Works

Updated September 2026 · 1673 words

Vietnam's visa rules are set by the Vietnam Immigration Department and they change more often than most travel advice admits. This page explains the process — which website is official, what to have ready, what causes most rejections, and what to check on the day you apply. It deliberately does not list fees, eligible nationalities or permitted lengths of stay, because those are exactly the details that go stale.

Read this first. Anything you read about Vietnam visas — including this page — can be out of date within months. Always confirm the current rules for your own passport on the official government portal before you pay anyone. If a detail here disagrees with the official portal, the portal is right.

Which website is actually official

Vietnam's electronic visa is handled by the Vietnam Immigration Department through a national portal. The department's own site carries a notice that, from 08:00 on 11 November 2024, the portal operates on two domains: evisa.gov.vn and thithucdientu.gov.vn. The older address, evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn, still loads and points visitors to the new domains.

Search results for "Vietnam visa" are crowded with agencies that look official, charge a service fee on top of the government fee, and in the worst cases collect your passport details without ever submitting anything. Two habits protect you: start from the government portal rather than an advertisement, and check that the domain you are typing is exactly one of the two above before you upload a passport scan.

The ways tourists enter Vietnam

In practice, most holiday visitors use one of four routes. Which one applies to you depends on your nationality, how long you are staying and where you enter, so treat this as a map of the options rather than advice for your passport.

  • Electronic visa (e-visa). Applied for online before you travel and used at the entries the portal lists. This is the route most independent travellers use, and the one this page focuses on.
  • Visa exemption. Some passports can enter for a limited stay without a visa at all, usually for tourism and often with a cap on total days. The current list is published by the immigration authorities and changes; check it rather than a blog.
  • Visa from a Vietnamese embassy or consulate. Useful if you are not eligible for an e-visa, if you need a longer stay, or if you want the visa settled well before you fly. Apply through the embassy that covers your country of residence.
  • Approval-letter arrangements. Agencies sell pre-approval paperwork for collecting a visa at the airport. This is a commercial service layered on top of official rules, and the rules around it have tightened over the years — if you consider it, verify on the official portal that it still applies to your nationality and entry point.

What to have ready before you start

  • Your passport, in front of you. Not a photo of it on your phone — the document itself, so you can copy the numbers exactly as printed.
  • A digital passport scan and a portrait photo that meet the specifications shown on the application form. Requirements on background, size and recency are listed there; follow them precisely rather than reusing an old photo.
  • Your dates and entry point. Know the airport or land crossing you will use and the date you arrive, plus where you will stay on the first night.
  • An address in Vietnam. A hotel name and address is enough for most applications; have the booking confirmation to hand.
  • A card that works internationally, and enough time. Applications are processed in working days, not hours, and public holidays slow everything down.

How the online application works

  1. Open the official portal and create an account, or start a new application as a guest if that option is offered.
  2. Enter your personal details exactly as they appear in the machine-readable zone of your passport — surname, given names and middle names in the right fields. This is the single most common source of problems.
  3. Upload the passport scan and the photograph the form asks for, and fill in your entry and exit dates and points.
  4. Pay the government fee directly on the portal and keep the receipt.
  5. Wait for processing, then download the result. Print at least one paper copy as well as saving it on your phone.
  6. Check the approved visa against your passport line by line: name spelling, passport number, dates, entry point. If something is wrong, contact the portal or the immigration office before you fly, not at the airport.

The mistakes that cause most rejections

  • Names that do not match the passport. Extra spaces, swapped given names or a missing middle name are enough to invalidate an approval.
  • Copying the date from a booking confirmation. Flight times and arrival dates cross time zones; write the date you actually land in Vietnam.
  • Choosing the wrong entry point. An e-visa is issued for specific ports of entry. If your flight is rerouted or you change your route, sort it out before you travel.
  • Applying too late. Allow working days, not hours, and add extra room around public holidays and Tet.
  • Using an unofficial website. You pay more, you hand over identity documents, and nobody answers when the approval does not arrive.
  • Letting the passport expire or be replaced in between. A new passport number invalidates a visa issued against the old one.

At the airport

Land with your printed approval and your passport in your hand, not in a checked bag. Immigration officers may ask to see an onward or return ticket, proof of where you are staying, and sometimes evidence that you can fund your stay; having those saved offline on your phone is usually enough. Keep the entry stamp page clean and undamaged, note the date you must leave by, and photograph the stamp — it is the record you will need if you extend your stay or come back.

If your plans change

Extending a stay or changing dates is handled by the immigration authorities in Vietnam, usually in Hanoi, Da Nang or Ho Chi Minh City, and often with the help of a licensed local agency or your hotel. It is a paperwork process with its own fees and processing times, so start well before your permitted stay runs out. Overstaying is treated seriously and carries a fine that grows with the number of days — if you realise you have miscalculated, deal with it at the immigration office rather than at the airport departure desk.

A five-minute checklist

  • Check your nationality's current status on the official portal, even if you were exempt last time.
  • Confirm your passport has plenty of validity left and at least one blank page.
  • Apply early enough to allow working days plus a holiday buffer.
  • Match every field to the passport itself, then proofread the approved visa.
  • Save the approval offline and print a copy.
  • Have your first-night address and onward ticket ready to show.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a visa for Vietnam as a tourist?

It depends entirely on your passport. Some nationalities can enter visa-free for a limited tourist stay, others need an e-visa applied for online, and a few need a visa from an embassy. Because the exemption list changes, check your own nationality on the official immigration portal rather than relying on advice written for a different passport.

Which website should I use to apply?

Only the official Vietnam Immigration Department portal. Its own notice states that from 08:00 on 11 November 2024 the portal operates at evisa.gov.vn and thithucdientu.gov.vn, with the older xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn address pointing there. Agencies that rank above the official site in search results add a service fee and are not the government.

How long does an e-visa take to be approved?

Processing is counted in working days, and the portal states the current timeframe when you apply. Add a buffer for weekends, Vietnamese public holidays and the Tet period, when offices close for several days. If your trip is within two weeks, apply today rather than tomorrow, and avoid booking a non-refundable flight first.

What if my personal details are wrong on the approved visa?

Do not travel and hope. A mismatch between the visa and your passport can mean being refused boarding or turned back at immigration. Contact the portal support or the immigration office that issued it and have it corrected before departure, even if that means a new application and a second fee.

Can I enter at a different airport than the one on my application?

An e-visa is issued for the entry points listed in the application, so changing your arrival airport without updating it is a risk. If your plans change, check the portal's rules and, where needed, submit a corrected application. Land crossings in particular have their own list of open ports of entry.

Should I use an agency to handle my visa?

A licensed local agency can genuinely help with extensions and unusual cases once you are in Vietnam, and hotels often assist with paperwork. For a straightforward tourist e-visa, though, applying yourself on the official portal is cheaper and gives you a direct record of the application, the fee and the result.
