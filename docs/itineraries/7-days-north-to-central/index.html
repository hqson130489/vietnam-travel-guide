7 Days from Hanoi to Hoi An: Northern Vietnam and the Central Coast

7 days · Updated September 2026 · 4219 words

Seven days is enough to see two very different Vietnams: the lakes, scooters and street kitchens of Hanoi in the north, then the lanterns, rice fields and beaches around Hoi An on the central coast. The trick is to keep the middle leg short. Fly it, and you spend your week in places rather than in transit.

Who this route suits

This is a first-trip route. It works best for travellers who want a taste of the north plus real time on the central coast, and who would rather see three places properly than five places badly.

It suits you if you are happy to take one short internal flight, you like walking and eating more than sitting on buses, and you accept that a seven-day trip means choosing. It suits couples, solo travellers, and families with children old enough to handle a long day out at Ninh Binh and a few hours in a car to Hue.

It is not the right route if Ha Long Bay is your main reason for coming. Overnight bay cruises generally need two days and a night of their own, and bolting one onto this week leaves you rushing everywhere else. Do the bay on a longer northern trip instead.

Nor is it a beach holiday. You get one beach afternoon at An Bang at most. If you want a week of sand, base yourself in Hoi An or Da Nang and skip the north entirely.

Why fly the middle leg, and when the train is better

The Reunification Express is one of the great train journeys in the region, and for a two-week trip it is the obvious choice. On a seven-day trip it is a trap.

Hanoi to Da Nang by rail takes the better part of a day and a night. Even the shorter Hanoi to Hue leg eats most of a day. A soft sleeper berth costs $30–55 (estimate, September 2026) to Hue and $35–65 (estimate, September 2026) to Da Nang, which looks like a bargain next to a flight at $45–120 (estimate, September 2026) one way. But the train does not save you money once you count the hotel night you paid for while travelling, the meals on board, and the day of your holiday you will never get back.

Flying takes a little over an hour in the air, and there are several flights a day between Hanoi and Da Nang, so you can leave after breakfast and be in Hoi An by mid-afternoon. For a seven-day trip that difference decides the whole shape of the week.

Take the train instead if you have ten days or more, if you want to break the journey at Hue or Phong Nha, if you are travelling in the wet season and want to avoid flight disruption, or if you simply want the experience of a sleeper train through the Vietnamese countryside. It is a good experience, just an expensive one in holiday time.

There is also a middle path that suits this route well: fly north to south as planned, and use the train for the short, scenic hop between Da Nang and Hue, where the line runs along the coast below the Hai Van Pass. That section is the best scenery on the whole line, and it turns a Hue day trip into part of the sightseeing.

Best months to travel

The north and the central coast do not share a climate, which is the single most common surprise on this route. Hanoi and Ninh Binh have a cooler, damper winter and a hot, wet summer. Da Nang, Hoi An and Hue are driest in the first half of the year and wettest from roughly September to December.

March and April

The most reliable window for combining both halves. Hanoi is warming up and often pleasant, Ninh Binh is green and comfortable for cycling, and the central coast is dry with warm sea. Book early, because this is also when everyone else wants to go.

October to early December

The north is at its best: cooler, drier, clear light, good walking weather in Hanoi and good photography in Ninh Binh. The risk moves south. Hoi An and Hue can see heavy rain and, in a bad year, flooding in the old town. If you pick this window, keep the Hoi An nights flexible and watch the forecast.

May to August

Hot and humid across the whole route, with afternoon downpours. It is workable if you start early, rest at midday and treat rain as a shower rather than a disaster. This is also typhoon season in the north, which can affect flights.

December to February

Hanoi can be genuinely cold and drizzly, sometimes down to around ten degrees, and Ninh Binh is often grey. The central coast is frequently wet as well. It is not a bad trip, but you will be packing a jacket as well as swimwear. Tet, the lunar new year, falls in late January or February and shifts each year: expect crowded transport, higher prices and some family-run places closed for several days around it.

Day by day

Day 1 — Land in Hanoi and get your bearings

Noi Bai airport sits about 45–60 minutes north of the city centre depending on traffic. A Grab car costs $12–25 (estimate, September 2026) and a pre-booked private car $20–35 (estimate, September 2026). Take the car and skip the taxi touts in the arrivals hall.

Base yourself in or near the Old Quarter for the first two nights. Drop your bags, walk to Hoan Kiem Lake, cross the red bridge to the small island temple, and let the traffic wash over you. The first hour in Hanoi is overwhelming for almost everyone; it settles down quickly.

Eat dinner somewhere busy and near your hotel. A bowl of pho, a plate of grilled pork with noodles, a beer on a plastic stool: this is the easiest introduction to the city you will get. Finish with an egg coffee if you need the caffeine to stay awake until a sensible bedtime.

Day 2 — Hanoi properly

Start early, while the lake is full of people doing tai chi and the streets are still cool. Walk the Old Quarter's craft streets, each of which traditionally sold one thing: silk, tin, shoes, herbs. Then pick two or three of the bigger sights rather than trying to tick off everything. The Temple of Literature, the prison museum, the fine arts museum and the Museum of Ethnology are all worth a half day each, and the Ethnology museum is the best single introduction to the country's ethnic groups.

In the afternoon, walk around the French Quarter and the opera house, or take a short taxi to West Lake for a quieter side of the city. In the evening, eat your way through the Old Quarter rather than committing to one restaurant: a few small plates in different places beats one big meal.

Book your Ninh Binh transport and your Hanoi to Da Nang flight today if you have not already. Both are cheaper and easier with a day or two of notice.

Day 3 — Ninh Binh, as a long day trip or an overnight

Ninh Binh is about two hours south of Hanoi by limousine van, at $12–20 (estimate, September 2026) each way. The scenery is the reason to come: limestone peaks standing straight out of flooded rice fields, rivers running through caves, and a stairway up to a viewpoint over the whole valley.

The classic day out is a boat ride at Tam Coc or Trang An, a stop at the old capital at Hoa Lu, and the climb up to the Hang Mua viewpoint if your legs are willing. A boat trip, a temple and a viewpoint is a full day; adding more turns it into a forced march.

If you do it as a day trip, you will be back in Hanoi in the evening and you will need a third night there. If you stay overnight in Tam Coc or Ninh Binh, you get the same sights with empty roads at sunrise and sunset, which is a much better experience, and you keep two nights in Hanoi instead of three. The trade-off is the next morning: Ninh Binh to Noi Bai airport is around two and a half hours, so you would need an afternoon flight rather than a morning one.

If you have any interest in photography or in cycling between rice fields, take the overnight. If you would rather not repack, do the day trip.

Day 4 — Fly to Da Nang and settle into Hoi An

Fly from Hanoi to Da Nang in the morning or around midday. The flight is a little over an hour, and the fare ranges from $45–120 (estimate, September 2026) one way depending on how early you book. Allow two hours at the airport for a domestic flight and check the baggage allowance, since the cheapest fares on some carriers are hand luggage only.

From Da Nang airport it is about 45 minutes by road to Hoi An: a Grab car at $12–25 (estimate, September 2026) or a private car at $20–35 (estimate, September 2026). Some Hoi An hotels include a pickup; ask when you book.

Spend the late afternoon walking the old town. Buy a ticket for the heritage houses and the Japanese covered bridge, then wander without a plan. After dark the streets are lit by lanterns and the river fills with small boats. It is busy, it is touristed, and it is still lovely. Eat cao lau or white rose for dinner, then walk back along the river.

Day 5 — Hoi An and the countryside

Give this day to Hoi An itself. A morning cooking class usually includes a market visit and a boat or bicycle ride, and it is the single best thing to do here. Alternatively, rent a bicycle and ride out through the rice fields and vegetable gardens to An Bang beach, about 15 minutes away, and swim before lunch.

The afternoon is for the old town's assembly halls, merchant houses and tailors. Hoi An is famous for clothes made to measure; a simple garment can be ready in a day, but a suit or a complex dress needs two fittings, so start early in your stay if this matters to you.

If you would rather see more, My Son, the Cham temple complex inland, is a half-day trip by car or organised tour. It is hot, exposed and busy by mid-morning, so go early.

Day 6 — Hue, as a day trip or an overnight

Hue, the old imperial capital, is roughly three to four hours from Hoi An by road, over the Hai Van Pass or through the tunnel. A private car costs $60–110 (estimate, September 2026) per car each way, so a day trip means paying for the round trip and spending six to eight hours in the car. It is doable, but it is a long day.

On the way, the pass has the best coastal views on the route, and the fishing village and lagoon below are worth a short stop. In Hue, the Imperial Citadel is the main sight, with the Thien Mu Pagoda and one or two of the royal tombs filling out the day.

Sleeping in Hue is the better version if your schedule allows. Travel up on Day 6 and spend the night, then either take the train over the pass back to Da Nang the next morning or drive. Be aware that this swaps one Hoi An night for a Hue night rather than adding a night, and that Hue to Da Nang airport is roughly two and a half to three hours, so your Day 7 flight should not be early.

If you only have the one day and you want a lighter version, skip Hue and use Day 6 for My Son or a beach day. Hue deserves more time than a rushed day trip.

Day 7 — Last morning and fly out of Da Nang

Keep the morning free for the old town at its quietest, a last coffee by the river, and any tailoring collection. Leave Hoi An about three hours before you need to be at Da Nang airport: 45 minutes for the drive, plus check-in time. The same Grab or private car prices apply.

If your flight is late in the evening, ask your hotel to store your bags and use the day for An Bang beach or the Marble Mountains on the way to the airport. Da Nang itself, with its long beach and the dragon bridge, is worth a couple of hours if you have them.

Where to stay, by area and price band

Prices below are per room per night. Dorm beds are priced per bed, so they are effectively per person.

Hanoi

  • Old Quarter — the best base for a first visit. Everything is walkable, the street food is on your doorstep, and budget guesthouses and 2-star hotels go for $18–35 (estimate, September 2026) with mid-range 3-star rooms at $40–75 (estimate, September 2026). The trade-off is noise, small rooms and windowless budget options. Ask for a room away from the street.
  • French Quarter and Ba Dinh — leafy, quieter, close to the museums and the mausoleum. Comfortable 4-star hotels run $90–150 (estimate, September 2026), and you are still only a short taxi ride from the Old Quarter.
  • West Lake — the expat side of town, with bigger rooms, lake views and the 5-star bracket at $170–380 (estimate, September 2026). It is 20–30 minutes from the Old Quarter, so it suits a slower trip than this one.

Ninh Binh

  • Tam Coc — guesthouses and small hotels among the karst and the rice fields, mostly $18–35 (estimate, September 2026) for a simple double and $40–75 (estimate, September 2026) for something more comfortable. This is where you want to be for sunrise and cycling.
  • Ninh Binh city — practical rather than pretty, but closest to the train station and the bus links if you are moving on by rail.

Hoi An

  • Inside the ancient town — the most atmospheric option, and the most expensive for what you get. Rooms in the pedestrian zone mean no car to the door, so you will walk the last stretch with your bags. In a wet autumn, riverside properties can flood.
  • Cam Chau and Tra Que — between town and beach, surrounded by rice fields and gardens, with good mid-range value at $40–75 (estimate, September 2026) and a 15-minute bicycle ride to the old town.
  • An Bang beach — for a beach-based stay, 10–15 minutes from town by bicycle or taxi, with a mix of guesthouses and small resorts.
  • Cam Nam island and the riverside — quieter, greener, and a short walk over the bridge to the lanterns.

Hue, if you stay the night

  • South bank, near the Citadel — walking distance to the imperial enclosure and the river, with a wide spread of 3-star and 4-star options.
  • Phu Hoi and the backpacker streets — cheap rooms, bars and travel agents, handy if you are arriving late and leaving early.

What to eat, and where

Hanoi street food

Hanoi is a city you eat your way through on foot. The Old Quarter's food streets are dense enough that you can graze all evening: the pho streets around Bat Dan and the market edges, the grilled pork and noodle shops near Hang Bac and Hang Be, and the bia hoi corners around Ta Hien and Luong Ngoc Quyen where the streets fill with people after dark.

Look for the dishes the north does best: pho with beef or chicken, bun cha with grilled pork and herbs, banh cuon steamed rice rolls, bun rieu crab noodle soup, and banh mi from a cart that has a queue. Eat where the seats are full and the menu is short. Bring small notes, because change for a large bill is not always available, and expect to share a table.

Hoi An

Two dishes belong to this town and are worth planning around. Cao lau is a bowl of thick noodles with braised pork, herbs and crisp fried croutons, and it is traditionally made with water from a particular well in town, which is why it tastes different elsewhere. White rose is a plate of small steamed dumplings, translucent and filled with prawn, folded to look like a flower and topped with crisp shallots.

Beyond those, look for chicken rice, banh xeo rice-flour crepes folded over prawn and bean sprouts, and grilled skewers from the riverfront stalls in the evening. The central market is best in the early morning, and a cooking class is the most useful souvenir you can bring home.

Hue

Hue eats differently from the rest of the country, thanks to its court kitchen. Bun bo Hue is the signature: a beef noodle soup with lemongrass and a chilli kick that makes pho look timid. Also look for banh khoai, a crisp stuffed rice pancake eaten wrapped in leaves and dipped, banh beo, small steamed rice discs with prawn, and nem lui, pork grilled on lemongrass skewers. Hue food comes in small portions, so order several dishes.

Drinks

Ca phe sua da, iced coffee with condensed milk, is the national fuel. Hanoi's egg coffee is worth trying once. Bia hoi, the light draught beer poured in the Old Quarter, is the cheapest drink in the country, and fresh beer in Hoi An does the same job by the river. Bottled water is cheap and you will drink a lot of it.

Common mistakes on this route

  • Squeezing in Ha Long Bay. An overnight cruise needs two days and a night of its own. Adding it to a seven-day route means cutting Hoi An short or losing a day in Hanoi. Save it for a longer northern trip.
  • Taking the train to save money. On a seven-day trip the sleeper costs you a full day of holiday. Fly, and spend the saved day in Hoi An.
  • Assuming one forecast covers the whole country. Hanoi in December can be cold and damp while Hoi An is warm, and the central coast is at its wettest in autumn. Check both ends of the route, not just the north.
  • Underestimating Hoi An to Hue. It is a three to four hour drive each way with the pass. As a day trip that is six to eight hours in a car. Sleep there, or skip it.
  • Booking a flight out of Da Nang too early on the last day. Hoi An to the airport is 45 minutes with no traffic, and much longer if there is any. Leave three hours before your flight.
  • Leaving the Hanoi to Da Nang flight late. Fares rise sharply inside the last week, and Tet and public holidays sell out. Book it with your hotels.
  • Booking a hotel inside the Hoi An pedestrian zone with heavy bags. Cars cannot reach the door. Ask where the drop-off point is before you book.
  • Carrying only large notes. Small vendors, market stalls and street food often cannot change a large bill. Keep a stack of small notes.
  • Packing for one climate. Even in the best months you want a light layer for Hanoi evenings, rain cover for the central coast, and shoes you can walk all day in.
  • Trying to do too much. Heat, humidity and traffic make three sights a full day, not six. Build in an afternoon with nothing planned.

Making it fit your dates

If you have more than seven days, the easiest additions are one night in Hue instead of a day trip, two nights at Ninh Binh with a bicycle, or a couple of beach days at An Bang before you fly home. If you have fewer, drop the Hue leg first, then Ninh Binh, and keep the nights in Hoi An. For a version of this route built around your own dates, see https://vibevietnamtour.com/.

Frequently asked questions

Is seven days enough for both Hanoi and Hoi An?

Yes, if you fly between them and accept three bases at most. You get two or three nights in Hanoi with a Ninh Binh day, three nights in Hoi An, and a Hue day trip if you want one. It feels unhurried as long as you resist adding Ha Long Bay or Da Lat. Any more stops and the week becomes a series of transfers.

Should I fly or take the train from Hanoi to Da Nang?

Fly on a seven-day trip. The train takes the better part of a day and a night, and a soft sleeper at $35–65 (estimate, September 2026) only looks cheaper until you count the lost hotel night and the lost day. Take the train if you have ten days or more, or for the short scenic hop between Da Nang and Hue.

Is a Ninh Binh day trip or an overnight better?

An overnight is better if you have the time. Tam Coc at sunrise, with the boats not yet out and the roads empty, is the version people remember. A day trip works well too and keeps you in one hotel, but you will share the main sights with the coach groups and spend four hours of the day in a van.

Should I sleep in Hue or visit it as a day trip from Hoi An?

Sleep there if you can. Hoi An to Hue is three to four hours each way, so a day trip means six to eight hours in a car and a rushed afternoon at the Citadel. Staying the night lets you see the tombs and the pagoda properly, and you can return by train over the Hai Van Pass.

What is the best time of year for this route?

March and April are the most reliable months for combining the north and the central coast. October to early December is excellent in Hanoi and Ninh Binh but wet in Hoi An and Hue. Avoid the Tet holiday in late January or February unless you have booked transport and hotels well ahead, as some family businesses close.

How much should I budget per person for this trip?

Roughly $316–586 (estimate, September 2026) at budget level, $622–1135 (estimate, September 2026) at mid-range and $1235–2340 (estimate, September 2026) in comfort, assuming two people share a room. The biggest variables are your hotel standard and how early you book the internal flight, which ranges from $45–120 (estimate, September 2026).
